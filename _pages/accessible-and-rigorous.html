The design challenge

Make professional learning
worth the time.

Gateway mathematics instructors work within coordinated courses, demanding teaching loads, and limited time for professional development. A useful learning experience has to respect their expertise and produce a change they can actually make.

I designed this toolkit around practical instructional problems: assessments that hide gaps in reasoning, course pages students struggle to navigate, and learning resources that assume everyone attended class.

The result is a seven-part toolkit with three standalone sessions, coaching and needs-assessment guidance, twelve working tools, and a plan for continued professional learning.

The learning experience

Three entry points. Real course artifacts.

Each session starts with a problem the instructor recognizes and ends with a usable artifact, peer review, and a dated next step. Participants can enter at the session that meets their current need.

Why this design

Agency with structure.

The toolkit combines backward design with a dialogue-based entry point. The intended performance and evidence shape the session, while the instructor’s own problem gives the work relevance.

Adult learning theory, Universal Design for Learning, and partnership-oriented coaching inform the design. Choice is purposeful: instructors can use built-in Canvas tools, templates, or AI-assisted HTML and still meet the same learning standard.

Decisions that matter

  • Problem before tool. Begin with the learning need, not a technology demonstration.
  • Work on an authentic artifact. Use something from the instructor’s own course.
  • Test the learner’s path. Check what someone can actually find, interpret, or begin.
  • Keep human verification central. Review AI output rather than treating a generated page as finished.
  • Plan beyond the workshop. Use follow-up evidence, reflection, and a voluntary professional learning community.

Implementation & reflection

What the pilot changed.

I facilitated a one-to-one adaptation of Your Page, Rebuilt with a gateway mathematics colleague on July 17, 2026. The experience informed the facilitation notes and subsequent revision of the session.

A useful tension emerged: the demonstration risked taking time away from the participant’s rebuild, but the participant also valued repeated examples.

The revision

Move additional examples into the guided rebuild, where they can respond to a specific question or sticking point. The goal is to preserve helpful explanation while protecting time for the participant to create.

This is the kind of iteration I want my design work to show: listen carefully, identify the tradeoff, and change the experience accordingly.

What this evidence does—and does not—show

The documented pilot supports a reflection on facilitation and a concrete design revision. It does not establish a measured improvement in student achievement or the effectiveness of all three sessions. The toolkit’s follow-up and evaluation structures are plans for gathering further evidence.
